Seven skill areas employers want

1. AI literacy for everyone

The biggest shortage isn't PhD-level research — it's ordinary employees who can use AI confidently and safely. UK employers now rank AI as the hardest skill to find, and they mean literacy and confidence in using AI in existing roles. Lightcast finds that almost all AI adoption is happening inside existing jobs rather than new AI job titles.

2. Prompt engineering and generative AI

Prompt engineering and large language models are among LinkedIn's fastest-growing skills for 2026. Coursera calls GenAI the most in-demand skill in its history, with enterprise GenAI enrolments up 234% and multimodal prompting and AI content creation rising quickly.

3. AI engineering and agentic AI

AI engineering tops LinkedIn's fastest-growing skills globally, alongside model tuning. The sharpest shift is towards agentic AI — systems that take actions, not just answer questions. Agentic skills grew by more than 280% in US postings, with a median advertised salary of $153,000 versus $141,000 for AI roles overall (Lightcast). In the UK, 57% of organisations plan to adopt agentic AI within three years.

4. Python, data and deployment

Python is the single most requested specialised skill in AI job postings. The fastest long-term growth is in deployment skills — cloud platforms such as AWS, scalability and workflow management. On the data side, data quality (+108%) and data cleansing (+103%) are among Coursera's fastest-growing skills.

5. Machine learning and NLP

Jobs requiring specialist AI skills such as machine learning grew 69%, compared with 9% across the whole job market (PwC). AI and machine learning specialists are among the WEF's fastest-growing jobs, and use of natural language processing in UK organisations has risen 34% in three years.

6. Governance, risk, compliance and security

Governance, risk management and compliance is one of LinkedIn's Skills on the Rise for 2026, and networks and cybersecurity is the WEF's second fastest-growing skill. With the EU AI Act's obligations phasing in, people who understand both AI and regulation are scarce.

7. Human skills that AI makes more valuable

Every report makes the same point: as AI does more, human judgement matters more. Critical-thinking enrolments grew 185% among Coursera's GenAI learners, as people shift to validating AI output. PwC highlights judgement, creativity, leadership and adaptability, and LinkedIn's 2026 list is full of leadership and communication skills.
