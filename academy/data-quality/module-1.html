Why data quality decides AI quality

The fastest way to a better model is usually better data. This module shows you what 'good data' means and why AI makes it matter more.

Learning objectives

  • Explain the data-centric view of AI
  • Assess data on six quality dimensions
  • Explain why data errors matter more in AI
  • Outline the data preparation workflow

1.1 Garbage in, garbage out

A model can only be as good as the data it learns from and the data it's given. Better algorithms rarely rescue bad data, but better data often rescues an ordinary algorithm. This idea — improving the data rather than endlessly tuning the model — is sometimes called data-centric AI.

Demand for these skills is growing fast: Coursera's 2026 job skills research reports data quality and data cleansing among the fastest-growing data skills, each roughly doubling in enrolments.

1.2 Six dimensions of data quality

DimensionQuestionExample problem
AccuracyDoes it reflect reality?Age recorded as 230
CompletenessIs anything missing?No signup date for some customers
ConsistencyIs the same thing recorded the same way?"UK", "uk", "United Kingdom", "U.K."
ValidityDoes it follow the rules and formats?A date of "2025-13-01" (month 13)
UniquenessAre there duplicates?The same customer appearing twice
TimelinessIs it up to date?Prices from last year used to predict demand

1.3 Why it matters more for AI

  • Models learn patterns — including errors. Mislabelled examples teach the wrong lesson.
  • Problems are invisible in outputs. A model trained on skewed data still produces confident predictions.
  • Errors scale. One bad rule in a spreadsheet affects one report; a model trained on it affects every decision it makes.
  • Unrepresentative data creates unfair outcomes for groups that are under-represented (Module 5).
  • Law requires it for high-risk AI. The EU AI Act's Article 10 sets data governance requirements for training, validation and testing data of high-risk systems (Module 6).

1.4 The data preparation workflow

  1. Understand the purpose and where the data comes from.
  2. Profile it — measure what's there (Module 2).
  3. Clean it — fix, remove or flag problems (Module 3).
  4. Label it, if the task needs labels (Module 4).
  5. Check representativeness and split it for training and testing (Module 5).
  6. Document and govern it (Module 6).

Try it: spot the problems

Before running any code, list every data quality problem you can see in the table, and which dimension each belongs to.

Show what to look for

Duplicate customer 103 (uniqueness); mixed date formats and an invalid month 13 (validity, consistency); missing signup date (completeness); five spellings of the UK and two of Ireland (consistency); ages 230 and −1 (accuracy, validity); missing age and spend (completeness); a spend of 12,000 that may be an outlier or error (accuracy); "no", "No", "yes", "YES" (consistency).

Key takeaways

  • Better data often beats a better algorithm.
  • Accuracy, completeness, consistency, validity, uniqueness and timeliness.
  • Models learn errors and scale them.
  • Understand → profile → clean → label → split → document.
