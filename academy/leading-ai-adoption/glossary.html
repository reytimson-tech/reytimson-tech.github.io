AI adoption glossary

Key terms from this course, in plain English.

No matching terms.

Acceptable-use policy
Rules telling staff which AI tools they may use, for what, with which data, and how to check and disclose AI output.
ADKAR
A change model: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement.
Adoption curve
The pattern by which new tools spread: early enthusiasts, a pragmatic majority, then sceptics.
AI champion
A volunteer in a team who receives extra training and time to help colleagues adopt AI and feed back issues.
AI literacy
The skills, knowledge and understanding needed to use AI effectively and responsibly; required of AI providers and deployers under Article 4 of the EU AI Act.
Automation bias
The tendency to accept automated output without sufficient scrutiny.
Case for change
A clear explanation of why change is needed, what will change and what won't.
Change management
Structured methods for helping people and organisations move to new ways of working.
Community of practice
An open group where people share techniques, examples and lessons about a topic.
Deskilling
Loss of skills when a task is routinely done by automation, making it harder to check or take over.
Guiding coalition
In Kotter's model, a group with the influence and commitment to lead a change.
J-curve
A dip in performance during the learning period before improvements appear.
Kotter's 8 steps
A model for organisational change, from creating urgency to anchoring change in culture.
Prompt library
A shared collection of tested prompts and examples for common tasks.
Psychological safety
A shared belief that people can take risks, ask questions and admit mistakes without punishment.
Pulse survey
A short, frequent survey to track sentiment and identify issues.
Reinforcement
Actions that sustain a change, such as recognition and building it into processes.
Shadow AI
Use of unapproved AI tools, often with company data, outside governance.
Task analysis
Breaking a job into its component tasks to decide which AI should support, automate or leave alone.
Workflow redesign
Changing how a process runs — steps, handovers and checks — to use AI effectively.
Works council
A body representing employees that, in some countries, must be consulted on changes affecting work.
