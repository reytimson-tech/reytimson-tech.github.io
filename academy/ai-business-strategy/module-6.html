Roadmaps and scaling

Scaling is where AI value is won or lost. This module turns your priorities into a roadmap and a scorecard leadership can steer by.

Learning objectives

  • Build a three-horizon AI roadmap
  • Plan what pilots need to reach production
  • Manage AI as a portfolio
  • Design a leadership scorecard

6.1 A roadmap in three horizons

HorizonFocusTypical outputs
Now (0–6 months)Foundations and quick winsApproved tools, policy, literacy training, 2–3 pilots, data audit
Next (6–18 months)Scale what works; redesign key processesProduction systems, shared platform, metrics dashboard
Later (18+ months)Strategic bets and new offeringsAI-enabled products, deeper automation, agents in core processes

6.2 From pilot to production

Many organisations get stuck after the pilot. Scaling needs things pilots skip: integration with core systems, security review, support and on-call, monitoring, training for all users, updated processes and job descriptions, and a budget line that doesn't end with the pilot. Plan these before the pilot finishes, so a "go" decision can move quickly.

6.3 Manage a portfolio, not projects

  • Balance quick wins, process transformations and strategic bets
  • Review the whole portfolio quarterly: scale, iterate or stop each item
  • Stop things — killing weak pilots frees money and people for strong ones
  • Reuse components (platforms, prompts, evaluation sets) across use cases

6.4 Reporting value

A simple quarterly AI scorecard for leadership:

ValueOutcomes delivered vs business case (cost saved, revenue, CSAT), by use case
AdoptionActive users, usage trend, coverage of target processes
PortfolioUse cases by stage; decisions made (scaled, stopped)
RiskIncidents, open risk actions, compliance status
CapabilityPeople trained, skills gaps, platform maturity

6.5 Keep the strategy current

AI capabilities, prices and regulations change every few months. Review the strategy at least twice a year: what new capabilities open up use cases that weren't feasible? Which assumptions in our business cases have changed? What did we learn from what we stopped? A strategy that doesn't change is probably not being used.

Activity: your one-page AI strategy

Bring the course together on one page: three priority outcomes; your top use cases by horizon (now / next / later); build-buy decisions; operating model; first governance controls; and the five scorecard measures you'll report quarterly.

See a worked example

Example headings: Outcomes — cut cost-to-serve 15%, grow repeat purchases 10%, zero AI-related compliance breaches. Now — support drafting pilot, approved assistant for all staff, AI literacy for managers. Next — scale support drafting, personalised bundles. Later — conversational shopping assistant. Operating model — hub and three spokes. Scorecard — cost per contact, repeat rate, active users, incidents, staff trained.

Key takeaways

  • Now, next, later: foundations, scale, strategic bets.
  • Plan production needs before the pilot ends.
  • Review the portfolio quarterly — and stop things.
  • Report value, adoption, portfolio, risk and capability.
  • Revisit the strategy at least twice a year.
