Build, buy and foundations

Most organisations buy more AI than they build. This module helps you decide which is which — and what foundations you need either way.

Learning objectives

  • Choose between buy, build-on-platform, build-custom and partner
  • Explain why data foundations matter
  • Outline key platform decisions
  • Evaluate AI vendors on data, security, quality and exit

4.1 Build, buy or partner?

OptionBest whenWatch out for
Buy (AI features in existing software, AI assistants)The need is common across companies; speed mattersLock-in, limited customisation, data terms
Build on a platform (model APIs, cloud AI services)You need integration with your data and processesSkills, ongoing maintenance, usage costs
Build custom (fine-tuned or own models)It's a core differentiator and you have unique dataCost, talent, time, responsibility for everything
PartnerYou lack skills now but want to build capabilityKnowledge transfer, dependency

A useful rule: buy for parity, build for differentiation. Most organisations mostly buy and build on platforms, and custom-build rarely.

4.2 Data is the foundation

Most AI value depends on your own data: documents for an assistant to answer from, records for a model to learn from, context for agents to act on. Before scaling, check that data is findable, accessible, good enough in quality, and governed — with clear ownership, access rules and lawful basis for use. See Data Quality & Preparation.

4.3 Platform choices

  • Model access — which providers and models, through which channels (direct API, cloud marketplace)
  • Retrieval and integration — connecting models to your documents and systems (see RAG and AI Agents)
  • Security and identity — single sign-on, permissions, logging, data residency
  • Operations — evaluation, monitoring and cost control (see LLMOps)

Avoid betting everything on one model: capabilities and prices change quickly. Designing so you can switch models is cheap insurance.

4.4 Evaluating vendors

AreaQuestions to ask
FitDoes it solve our use case on our data? Can we trial it with real examples?
DataIs our data used for training? Where is it stored and for how long? Can we delete it?
Security & complianceCertifications, access controls, audit logs, regulatory support
Quality & safetyHow is accuracy measured? What guardrails exist? How are incidents handled?
Cost & exitPricing at our volume, price-change terms, data export, switching costs

4.5 Skills and talent

You'll need a blend: product owners who understand the business, engineers who can integrate AI safely, data specialists, risk and legal partners, and — most of all — AI-literate staff across the business. Upskilling existing people who know the work is usually faster than hiring. See Leading AI Adoption & Change.

Activity: build, buy or partner

For three use cases, decide build, buy, build-on-platform or partner. Justify each in one sentence using differentiation, data, speed and skills.

See a worked example

Example: Meeting summaries → buy (common need, built into existing tools). Support drafting on our knowledge base → build on a platform (needs our data and CRM integration). Pricing optimisation using 10 years of sales data → build custom with a partner (differentiating, unique data, skills gap).

Key takeaways

  • Buy for parity, build for differentiation.
  • Your data — findable, accessible, good, governed — is the foundation.
  • Design to switch models as the market changes.
  • Ask vendors about data use, security, quality and exit.
  • Upskilling people who know the work is often fastest.
