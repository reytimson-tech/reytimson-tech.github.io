Inventories, risk and impact assessment

This is where governance gets practical: finding your AI, classifying it, assessing what could go wrong, and holding vendors to account.

Learning objectives

  • Find AI across the organisation and record it in an inventory
  • Design a proportionate risk-tiering scheme
  • Assess and treat AI risks
  • Choose and combine the right impact assessments
  • Run AI vendor due diligence

5.1 Start with an AI inventory

You can't govern what you can't see. An AI inventory (or register) is the foundation of every governance programme — and the EU AI Act Fundamentals course recommends it as the first compliance step.

Find AI through: surveys of business units; procurement and contract records; software and SaaS asset lists (many tools have added AI features); expense claims for AI subscriptions; IT network data; and data science project lists.

5.2 Risk tiering

Classify each use case so governance effort is proportionate. A simple internal scheme:

TierTypical criteriaGovernance
ProhibitedBanned by law or contrary to policyNot allowed
HighSignificant decisions about people; safety; legally high-risk; sensitive data at scale; customer-facing autonomyFull impact assessment, committee approval, testing, monitoring, periodic audit
MediumInternal decision support; customer-facing content with human review; personal dataStandard assessment, owner sign-off, monitoring
LowProductivity uses with no personal data or decisions about peopleApproved-tool list, acceptable use policy

5.3 Assessing risk

For each system, assess risks across categories — for example the NIST trustworthiness characteristics or the GenAI Profile's twelve risks — rating likelihood and impact, then deciding treatment: mitigate, transfer, avoid or accept, within the organisation's risk appetite.

5.4 Impact assessments

Several assessments may apply. Combine them where you can to avoid duplication:

AssessmentFocusWhen

5.5 Third-party and vendor due diligence

Most organisations buy far more AI than they build. Key questions for AI vendors:

  • What does the AI do, what model(s) does it use, and who provides them?
  • How is our data used, stored and retained? Is it used for training?
  • What testing has been done for accuracy, bias, robustness and security? Can we see results?
  • What documentation is available — model cards, transparency notes, instructions for use?
  • What certifications do you hold (ISO/IEC 42001, ISO 27001, SOC 2)?
  • How will you support our legal obligations (e.g. EU AI Act deployer duties, DPIAs)?
  • How are incidents notified? How are model changes communicated?
  • What contractual commitments — liability, IP indemnities, audit rights — will you give?

Key takeaways

  • The inventory is the foundation — look in procurement, SaaS lists, expenses and surveys.
  • Tier risk to make governance proportionate.
  • Assess likelihood and impact; mitigate, transfer, avoid or accept.
  • Combine AIIA, DPIA, FRIA, equality and security assessments where possible.
  • Ask vendors about data use, testing, documentation, certifications, incidents and contracts.
