Responsible AI glossary

Key terms from this course, in plain English.

Accountability
Identifiable people and organisations answering for an AI system's design, use and effects, with routes to redress.
AI ethics
The study of moral questions raised by AI: what should be built, for whom and with what trade-offs.
Calibration
A fairness property where a given risk score means the same likelihood for every group.
Contestability
The ability of affected people to challenge AI-influenced decisions and obtain human review.
Counterfactual testing
Changing one attribute in an input (e.g. a name) and comparing outputs to detect bias.
Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)
An assessment of risks to individuals from processing personal data, required under GDPR for high-risk processing.
Datasheet for datasets
Documentation of how a dataset was collected, its composition, intended uses and known issues.
Demographic parity
A fairness definition requiring equal rates of positive outcomes across groups.
Disaggregated evaluation
Measuring a model's performance separately for different groups rather than only overall.
Equal opportunity
A fairness definition requiring equal true-positive rates across groups; 'equalised odds' also requires equal false-positive rates.
Ethics washing
Publishing ethical principles without meaningfully changing practice.
Explainability
The ability to give meaningful reasons for an AI output.
Fail-safe design
Designing systems so that failures lead to a safe state, such as deferring to a human.
Historical bias
Bias that arises because data accurately reflects an unfair world.
Indirect discrimination
Under the Equality Act 2010, a practice that puts a protected group at a particular disadvantage without objective justification.
Interpretable model
A model whose decision-making can be directly understood, such as a simple scorecard.
Measurement bias
Bias from using a poor proxy for the real quantity of interest.
Model card
Documentation of a model's intended use, performance across groups, limitations and ethical considerations.
Proxy
A feature that indirectly encodes a protected characteristic, such as postcode.
Representation bias
Bias from under-representation of some groups in data.
Responsible AI
Designing, building, buying and using AI in ways that are safe, fair, transparent, accountable and rights-respecting.
Responsible Scaling Policy
Anthropic's framework setting safety measures that scale with model capabilities.
Robustness
The ability of a system to perform well under unusual, noisy or adversarial conditions.
System card
A document published with a frontier model describing its capabilities, safety evaluations and mitigations.
Transparency note
Microsoft's documentation explaining how an AI service works, its intended uses and limitations.
