Why validation matters

As AI drafts more of our work, the ability to judge it becomes one of the most valuable skills you can have.

Learning objectives

  • Explain why validation is a growing, valuable skill
  • Describe why fluent AI output is hard to judge
  • Explain automation bias and its two kinds of error
  • Match the level of checking to the stakes

1.1 From creator to validator

As generative AI takes on more first drafts, more of our value comes from judging work rather than producing every word. Coursera's 2026 job skills research found critical-thinking enrolments growing sharply among generative AI learners, as people shift towards being expert validators of AI. PwC's AI jobs research finds that AI is making human skills like judgement more valuable, not less.

In Anthropic's 4D AI Fluency Framework, this skill is called Discernment: evaluating AI outputs, the process that produced them, and the AI's behaviour, with a critical eye.

1.2 Why AI output is hard to judge

  • Fluency hides errors. AI writes confidently and grammatically whether it's right or wrong. We instinctively trust polished prose.
  • Errors are plausible. A hallucinated statistic looks just like a real one. A fake court case has a realistic name and citation.
  • Volume and speed. AI produces far more than we can carefully read, tempting us to skim.
  • It's usually right. The better AI gets, the easier it is to stop checking — and the more damaging the rare error.

1.3 Automation bias and complacency

Automation bias is the tendency to favour suggestions from automated systems, even over contradictory evidence. Research in aviation, healthcare and other fields has found people miss errors more often when a reliable-seeming system is involved — both by failing to notice problems the system didn't flag (omission errors) and by following wrong suggestions (commission errors).

The irony of good AI

A tool that's right 95% of the time can be more dangerous than one that's right 60% of the time — because people stop checking the first one.

1.4 What validation means

Validation isn't re-doing the whole task. It's applying proportionate checks, focused on where errors are likely and costly:

StakesExampleProportionate check
LowBrainstorm for a team socialQuick read for sense and tone
MediumInternal report summaryCheck key facts and figures against the source; spot-check quotes
HighCustomer advice, legal or financial content, published material, decisions about peopleVerify every claim, figure and reference; expert review; documented sign-off

1.5 A validation mindset

  • Treat AI output as a draft from a talented but unreliable colleague
  • Decide before reading what you'll check
  • Be most sceptical where the output matches what you hoped to hear
  • Separate "sounds right" from "is verified"

Key takeaways

  • Discernment — judging AI output — is a core AI fluency skill.
  • Fluency hides errors; plausible errors are the hardest to spot.
  • Reliable-seeming systems make people stop checking.
  • Check proportionately: more where errors are likely and costly.
