Validating AI output: glossary

Key terms from this course, in plain English.

No matching terms.

Automation bias
The tendency to favour automated suggestions over other evidence, leading to missed errors (omission) or following wrong advice (commission).
Citation check
Confirming a source exists, the details are correct, it says what's claimed, and it is current and authoritative.
Commission error
Following an automated system's incorrect suggestion.
Confirmation bias
Favouring information that supports what we already believe.
Contextual error
When AI ignores, misreads or mixes up the material it was given.
Correlation vs causation
Two things moving together doesn't mean one causes the other.
Counterfactual check
Testing for bias by changing one attribute — such as a name or age — and comparing outputs.
Discernment
In Anthropic's 4D Framework, the competency of critically evaluating AI output, process and behaviour.
Erasure
When groups, perspectives or examples are missing from AI output.
Fabrication
AI inventing facts, statistics, quotes, people or sources.
Hallucination
Plausible but false or unsupported AI output.
Human oversight
Meaningful human monitoring and control of AI, with the competence, time and authority to intervene.
Lateral reading
Judging a source by leaving it to see what other reliable sources say about it.
Misattribution
Citing a real source for something it doesn't say.
Omission error
Missing a problem because an automated system didn't flag it.
Percentage point
The arithmetic difference between two percentages (4% to 5% is one percentage point, a 25% increase).
Proxy variable
A data point, such as postcode, that indirectly reflects a protected characteristic.
Rubber-stamping
Approving automated outputs without genuine scrutiny.
Sanity check
A quick test of whether a result is plausible — magnitude, units, totals and direction.
SIFT
A fact-checking method: Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace claims to the original.
Sycophancy
AI's tendency to agree with or flatter the user, including accepting false premises.
Triangulation
Confirming a claim with multiple independent, reliable sources.
