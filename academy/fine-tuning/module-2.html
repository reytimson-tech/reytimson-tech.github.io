Fine-tuning methods

There are many ways to adapt a model. Knowing what each changes — and what it needs — helps you pick the cheapest one that works.

Learning objectives

  • Match SFT, DPO, RFT, continued pre-training and distillation to use cases

2.1 Full fine-tuning

Update all of the model's parameters. Maximum flexibility, but for modern LLMs it needs a lot of GPU memory, produces a full-size copy of the model for every tune, and risks catastrophic forgetting — losing general abilities as the model specialises.

2.2 Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)

PEFT methods freeze the original weights and train a small number of extra parameters. The most popular is LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation): alongside a frozen weight matrix, it learns two small matrices whose product is a low-rank "update". Typically well under 1% of the parameters are trained.

  • Much less memory and faster training.
  • Tiny adapter files (megabytes, not gigabytes) — you can keep many adapters for one base model and swap them.
  • Less forgetting, because the base weights are untouched.
  • QLoRA goes further by loading the frozen base model in 4-bit precision, making it possible to tune fairly large models on a single GPU.

2.3 What you're optimising for: training objectives

MethodTraining dataGood for
Supervised fine-tuning (SFT)Examples of inputs with ideal outputsFormats, classification, extraction, style, instruction following
Preference tuning — e.g. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)For each prompt, a preferred and a rejected responseTone, helpfulness, what to emphasise in summaries
Reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT)Tasks plus a grader that scores answersImproving reasoning on tasks with checkable answers
Continued pre-trainingLarge amounts of unlabelled domain textSpecialist vocabulary (e.g. legal, clinical), before SFT
DistillationOutputs from a larger "teacher" modelTeaching a small model to imitate a big one on a task (Module 6)

These are the same ingredients model developers use in post-training (see How AI Works, Module 5) — applied to your narrow task.

2.4 Choosing a base model

  • Start from an instruction-tuned model, not a raw base model, for most tasks
  • Prefer the smallest model that reaches your quality bar after tuning
  • Check the licence permits your commercial use and fine-tuning
  • Check language and domain coverage
  • Consider where it will be served and at what cost

Key takeaways

  • Full fine-tuning updates everything: costly and risks forgetting.
  • LoRA trains small adapter matrices while the base stays frozen; QLoRA adds 4-bit loading.
  • SFT learns from ideal outputs; DPO from preferred vs rejected; RFT from a grader.
  • Start from the smallest suitable instruction-tuned model with a permissive licence.
