AI governance glossary

Key terms from this course, in plain English.

No matching terms.

Acceptable use policy (AUP)
Rules for staff on which AI tools may be used, with what data, and how.
AI governance
The structures, policies, processes and controls that ensure AI is used in line with values, risk appetite and law.
AI impact assessment (AIIA)
An assessment of an AI system's potential impacts on individuals, groups and society (see ISO/IEC 42005).
AI incident
An event where an AI system causes or nearly causes harm, such as a harmful output, discriminatory outcome, data leak or malfunction.
AI inventory
A register of all AI systems an organisation develops, buys or uses, with owners, purposes and risk classifications.
AI management system (AIMS)
The organisational system of policies, objectives and processes for governing AI, specified by ISO/IEC 42001.
AI Security Institute
The UK government body that evaluates the safety and security of advanced AI models.
Annex A (ISO/IEC 42001)
Reference controls organisations select based on their AI risk assessment.
Confabulation
NIST's term for generative AI producing confidently stated but false content.
DPIA
Data Protection Impact Assessment, required under GDPR for high-risk processing of personal data.
Federated governance
A hub-and-spoke model where a central team sets standards and business-unit representatives handle routine reviews.
FRIA
Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment, required under the EU AI Act for certain deployers of high-risk AI.
Generative AI Profile
NIST AI 600-1 (July 2024), applying the AI RMF to generative AI and identifying twelve risks.
ISO/IEC 23894
International guidance on AI risk management.
ISO/IEC 42001
The international standard (2023) specifying requirements for a certifiable AI management system.
ISO/IEC 42005
The international standard (2025) on AI system impact assessment.
NIST AI RMF
The US National Institute of Standards and Technology's voluntary AI Risk Management Framework, structured around Govern, Map, Measure and Manage.
Risk appetite
The amount and type of risk an organisation is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives.
Risk tiering
Classifying AI uses by risk level so governance is proportionate.
Statement of Applicability
An ISO management-system document recording which controls apply and why.
Three lines model
A governance model: first line owns risk, second line oversees and challenges, third line (internal audit) provides independent assurance.
Vendor due diligence
Assessing a supplier's AI for data use, testing, documentation, security, certifications and contractual commitments.
