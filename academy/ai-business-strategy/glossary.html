AI strategy glossary

Key terms from this course, in plain English.

No matching terms.

AI literacy
The knowledge and skills people need to use AI effectively and responsibly, and to understand its limits.
AI system inventory
A register of the AI systems an organisation uses or builds, with owners, purposes and risk ratings.
Baseline
The measured performance before a change, used to judge whether the change worked.
Build vs buy
The decision whether to develop an AI capability in-house, build on a platform, buy a product or partner.
Business case
A structured argument for an investment: what changes, benefits, full costs, risks and how success will be measured.
Centre of excellence (CoE)
A central team that sets AI standards, runs shared platforms and supports business units.
Change management
Helping people and processes adapt to a new way of working so the benefits are realised.
Feasibility
How achievable a use case is, considering data, technical complexity, integration, risk and change.
Hub and spoke
An operating model where a central hub provides platforms and standards and business-unit spokes deliver use cases.
KPI
Key performance indicator: a measure that tracks progress towards an important objective.
Lock-in
Dependence on one vendor that makes switching costly or difficult.
Operating model
How an organisation structures teams, decisions, processes and platforms to deliver AI.
Pilot
A time-boxed trial with real users and data, designed to test a hypothesis and support a scale, iterate or stop decision.
Portfolio
The full set of AI initiatives, managed together to balance value, risk and learning.
Proportionate governance
Oversight matched to the risk of each AI use, so low-risk uses move quickly and high-risk ones get deeper review.
Quick win
A use case with high value and high feasibility — a good place to start.
Risk appetite
The level and type of risk an organisation is willing to accept in pursuit of its goals.
Roadmap
A plan sequencing initiatives over time, often in horizons such as now, next and later.
ROI
Return on investment: the benefits of an initiative relative to its costs.
Scorecard
A concise report of key measures used by leadership to steer a programme.
Shadow AI
Staff using AI tools that haven't been approved, often with company data, outside governance.
Strategic bet
A high-value but harder use case that needs foundations or investment before it can succeed.
Use case
A specific business problem or opportunity where AI is applied, with users, data and a measurable outcome.
Value lever
A way AI creates value: productivity and cost, revenue and growth, experience, or risk and quality.
Vanity metric
A number that looks good (such as logins) but doesn't show whether outcomes improved.
