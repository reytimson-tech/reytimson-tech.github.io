Labelling and annotation

For supervised learning, labels are the lesson. This module shows how to make them consistent, efficient and trustworthy.

Learning objectives

  • Explain how label quality limits model quality
  • Write effective labelling guidelines
  • Measure inter-annotator agreement with Cohen's kappa
  • Choose efficient labelling techniques, including LLM assistance
  • Detect label errors and treat labellers responsibly

4.1 Why labels matter

Supervised learning learns from labels — the "right answers" attached to examples: is this email spam, which category is this ticket, is this X-ray abnormal? Inconsistent or wrong labels put a ceiling on model quality, and evaluation against wrong labels gives misleading scores.

4.2 Write labelling guidelines

  • A clear definition of every label, with examples and counter-examples
  • Rules for ambiguous and borderline cases — and an "unsure" option
  • What to do when information is missing
  • Worked examples reviewed by experts
  • A version number — update the guidelines as you learn

4.3 Measure agreement between labellers

Have two or more people label the same sample independently and measure how often they agree. Cohen's kappa measures agreement between two labellers while correcting for agreement expected by chance: 1 is perfect, 0 is no better than chance. Low agreement usually means the guidelines — not the labellers — need work.

Python · inter-annotator agreementfrom sklearn.metrics import cohen_kappa_score

annotator_a = ["billing", "delivery", "product", "delivery", "other", "billing", "product", "delivery"]
annotator_b = ["billing", "delivery", "product", "billing",  "other", "billing", "delivery", "delivery"]

agreement = sum(a == b for a, b in zip(annotator_a, annotator_b)) / len(annotator_a)
kappa = cohen_kappa_score(annotator_a, annotator_b)
print(f"Raw agreement: {agreement:.0%}   Cohen's kappa: {kappa:.2f}")

# Which items do they disagree on? Discuss them and refine the guidelines.
print([i for i, (a, b) in enumerate(zip(annotator_a, annotator_b)) if a != b])

4.5 Finding label errors

Even well-run projects contain label errors. Useful checks: examples where a trained model confidently disagrees with the label; duplicates or near-duplicates with different labels; and spot audits of random samples by an expert.

    "text":  ["Where is my order?", "Where is my order?", "Charged twice", "Refund please"],
    "label": ["delivery",           "billing",            "billing",       "billing"],
})
conflicts = tickets.groupby("text")["label"].nunique()
print(conflicts[conflicts > 1])     # same text, different labels -> review

4.6 Labellers are people

Much labelling work is outsourced. Responsible practice includes fair pay, clear instructions, reasonable workloads, and protection and support for people asked to review distressing content.

Key takeaways

  • Wrong labels cap model quality and distort evaluation.
  • Guidelines need definitions, examples, edge-case rules and versions.
  • Low agreement usually means unclear guidelines.
  • LLM-proposed labels need human review and measured agreement.
  • Look for conflicts, confident model disagreements and audit samples.
