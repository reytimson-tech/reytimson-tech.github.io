Privacy, safety and human agency

Responsible AI protects people's data, keeps them safe and keeps them in control. This module covers those principles and AI's wider impacts.

Learning objectives

  • Identify privacy risks at each stage of the AI lifecycle
  • Apply data protection principles and DPIAs to AI
  • Explain safety, robustness and fail-safe design
  • Describe meaningful human agency and oversight
  • Consider inclusiveness, environmental and societal impacts

5.1 Privacy

AI systems are hungry for data, and that creates privacy risks across the lifecycle:

  • Collection: scraping or repurposing personal data people didn't expect to be used this way.
  • Training: models can memorise and later reveal personal data from their training sets.
  • Inference: AI can infer sensitive information — health, sexuality, political views — from seemingly harmless data.
  • Use: staff pasting personal data into AI tools; chat logs stored and reviewed.

Data protection law (UK GDPR, EU GDPR) applies throughout. Key principles include lawfulness, fairness and transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; and security. A Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is typically required for high-risk processing, which many AI uses involve.

Privacy-enhancing techniques include anonymisation and pseudonymisation, synthetic data, differential privacy and federated learning — each with trade-offs.

5.2 Safety and robustness

A safe AI system works reliably in the conditions it's used in, behaves sensibly in conditions it wasn't designed for, and resists misuse. Key ideas:

ConceptMeaning
ReliabilityConsistent performance in expected conditions
RobustnessHolding up under unusual, noisy or adversarial inputs
Fail-safe designWhen it fails, it fails in a safe way — e.g. defers to a human
Misuse preventionGuardrails against harmful use — e.g. generating dangerous instructions or abusive content
MonitoringDetecting drift, incidents and new failure modes after launch

For the most capable frontier models, developers have published safety frameworks — such as Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy and OpenAI's Preparedness Framework — that set capability thresholds requiring stronger safeguards. The AI Security & Safety course covers security in depth.

5.3 Human agency and oversight

Responsible AI should support human decision-making and autonomy, not quietly replace or manipulate it:

  • Meaningful oversight — people with the competence, time and authority to intervene.
  • No manipulation — AI shouldn't exploit psychological weaknesses. Manipulative AI causing significant harm is prohibited under the EU AI Act.
  • Avoiding over-reliance — design that encourages users to think critically, not defer automatically.
  • Choice — where appropriate, people can opt for a human alternative.

5.4 Inclusiveness and accessibility

Microsoft's principles call out inclusiveness: AI should empower everyone, including people with disabilities. AI can be a powerful accessibility tool (captions, screen readers, image descriptions) — and can exclude people when, for example, speech recognition fails on atypical speech. Include diverse users in design and testing.

5.5 Environmental and societal impact

  • Environment: training and running large models uses significant energy and water for data centres. Choose appropriately sized models, and weigh benefits against costs.
  • Work: AI changes tasks and jobs. Responsible adoption involves workers, invests in skills and considers who bears the costs.
  • Information ecosystem: generative AI can spread misinformation and erode trust in genuine content. Labelling and provenance standards help.
  • Creators: questions about training on copyrighted works, consent and compensation remain contested and are being addressed by courts and lawmakers.

Key takeaways

  • AI creates privacy risks in collection, training, inference and use.
  • Data protection principles and DPIAs apply to AI.
  • Safe systems are reliable, robust, fail safely and resist misuse.
  • Frontier developers publish safety frameworks with capability thresholds.
  • AI should support human autonomy, include everyone and weigh environmental and social costs.
